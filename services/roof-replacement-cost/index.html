How a New Roof Is Priced Around Metro Detroit

Every roof is different, which is why we don't publish price lists. For most single-family homes across Wayne, Oakland, and Macomb counties, the price of an asphalt shingle roof replacement comes down to a handful of things: how big the roof is, how steep and cut-up it is, what has to come off, and what goes back on. A smaller, simple ranch with one clean roof plane is a very different job from a larger two-story home with steep pitches, multiple dormers, and premium shingles.

Roofers measure the job by the 'square', and one square covers 100 square feet of roof surface. Most metro Detroit homes work out to somewhere between 15 and 30 squares once you account for pitch and overhangs, which is why two houses that look similar from the street can carry very different price tags. Smaller repairs are a different story: patching a few slipped shingles, sealing a flashing leak, or fixing a modest wind-damaged section is priced on the work involved, not the whole roof.

  • Full replacement: priced on roof size, pitch, tear-off, and the shingle line you choose
  • Common roof repairs: priced on the problem, the access, and the materials involved
  • Metal roofing: costs more upfront than asphalt, with a much longer service life
  • Priced by the square (100 sq ft) — most homes here are 15-30 squares
  • The only reliable price is one based on your roof: call (313) 236-4558 for a price on your job

What Actually Drives Your Roof Replacement Price

Two homes on the same block can end up with very different prices, and it's rarely about markup. It's about what the roof is made of and what it takes to do it right. The biggest lever is simple size: more squares means more material and more labor. After that, the details start to add up, and Michigan's climate quietly raises the stakes on a few of them.

Steep pitches slow the crew down and require extra safety setup. Tearing off two or three old layers costs more than a single layer and more disposal. And once the old shingles come off, we sometimes find soft or rotted decking or worn flashing that has to be replaced to give you a roof that actually holds up to freeze-thaw cycles and heavy snow load — we'll always show you before we touch it.

  • Roof size in squares — the single largest factor in the total
  • Pitch and complexity — steep slopes, dormers, valleys, and hips add labor
  • Tear-off layers — removing two or three old layers costs more than one
  • Shingle grade — architectural and premium lines cost more but shed Michigan weather better
  • Decking and flashing — rotted plywood or worn flashing found at tear-off needs replacing
  • Penetrations — chimneys, skylights, vents, and pipe boots each need careful sealing
  • Accessibility — tight lots, steep driveways, and landscaping affect setup and cleanup

Shingles or Metal? Matching the Material to Michigan Weather

The material you choose is where budget meets longevity. Architectural asphalt shingles are the workhorse of metro Detroit roofs for good reason — they balance cost, curb appeal, and real resistance to the spring wind and hail storms, ice dams, and humid summers we all know too well. Stepping up to a premium impact-rated or high-wind shingle costs more per square but buys you extra peace of mind through lake-effect moisture and hard winters.

Standing-seam and metal roofing costs considerably more upfront than asphalt, but it shrugs off heavy snow load, resists ice dams beautifully, and can last decades longer. Whichever direction you lean, we back our installs with manufacturer-backed material warranties and our own workmanship guarantee, and we'll walk you through the trade-offs in plain English so the choice fits your budget, not ours.

  • Architectural asphalt — the practical, cost-effective choice for most homes here
  • Premium impact- or wind-rated shingles — more upfront, extra protection against hail and gusts
  • Metal roofing: a bigger upfront investment, excellent for snow shedding and ice-dam resistance
  • Proper ventilation and ice-and-water shield — quiet upgrades that fight ice dams and attic moisture
  • All installs backed by manufacturer material warranties and our workmanship guarantee

Repair or Replace? Running the Numbers Both Ways

Not every roof problem needs a full replacement, and we'll never tell you it does when it doesn't. If your roof is generally sound and you're dealing with an isolated issue, such as a handful of shingles the last windstorm peeled off, a single flashing leak, or a ceiling stain from one bad spot, a targeted repair often buys you years of good service for far less than a new roof.

The math shifts when repairs start stacking up. If your roof is aging, leaking in more than one place, or you're patching it every spring after the freeze-thaw season, pouring money into repeat repairs can quietly cost more than a replacement that resets the clock and restores your full warranty coverage. We'll give you our honest read after we're up on the roof — repair if that's the smart call, replace only when it truly earns its keep.

  • Lean toward repair: isolated leak, minor storm damage, an otherwise sound roof with life left
  • Lean toward replacement: widespread leaks, aging shingles, or repeat repairs every season
  • Watch the tipping point: several repairs in a short window often outrun the cost of replacing
  • A replacement resets your warranty coverage and your peace of mind for decades

Cost Factors — FAQs

How much does a roof replacement cost in metro Detroit?

It depends on your roof, which is why we don't publish price lists. The price of an asphalt shingle replacement comes down to roof size in squares, pitch and complexity, how many old layers we tear off, the condition of the decking and flashing underneath, and the shingle grade you choose. Metal roofing costs more upfront than asphalt. Call (313) 236-4558 for a price on your job.

How long does a roof replacement take, and how soon can you start?

Most residential roofs are torn off and replaced in one to three days once we begin, weather permitting, since Michigan skies can push a schedule. Scheduling depends on the season and our current workload, though same-day inspections are sometimes available when our schedule allows. The fastest way to lock in a start date is to get in touch so we can get you on the calendar.

Should I repair my roof or replace it?

If your roof is generally sound with an isolated problem, such as a few storm-lifted shingles or a single flashing leak, a targeted repair often makes sense. But if it's aging, leaking in several spots, or needing patches every spring, repeat repairs can outrun the cost of replacing. We'll give you an honest read after inspecting it in person.

Do you offer financing for a new roof?

Yes. Financing is available on approved credit, so you can protect your home now and spread the cost over time instead of paying all at once. We'll walk you through the options in plain terms alongside the written price for your job, with no pressure. Reach out and we'll help you find an approach that fits your budget.

Will insurance pay for my roof replacement?

If a windstorm or hail event caused your damage, your homeowner's insurance may cover part of the work. We help document the damage and work with your adjuster through the claims process. Be aware that your deductible is your responsibility to pay. We'll always be honest about that, and we never promise that insurance will cover the whole job.

Why does my roof cost more than my neighbor's?

Similar-looking homes often price differently. Steeper pitches, more roof squares, extra layers to tear off, premium shingles, replacing rotted decking or worn flashing, and features like chimneys and skylights all move the number. A tight lot or difficult access can add setup time too. When we price your job, we break down exactly what your roof needs so the price makes sense.
